Florida’s golf cart and LSV rules are in state law, which the Florida Legislature publishes. Here’s a plain-English summary.

State cart laws · Updated October 2026

Golf cart laws in Florida

In Florida, golf carts can use only the public roads that a county or city has opened to them and signed. Low-speed vehicles (LSVs) are a separate class: they’re titled, registered and insured, and can use streets posted 35 mph or less.

Do you need a driver’s license to drive a golf cart in Florida?

Not always, for golf carts. Drivers under 18 need a learner’s permit or a driver’s license; drivers 18 and over need a government-issued photo ID. Many towns are stricter and require a driver’s license. LSV drivers always need a valid driver’s license.

How old do you have to be to drive a golf cart in Florida?

Florida doesn’t set a minimum age number for golf carts. Under 18, the driver needs a learner’s permit or a driver’s license; 18 and over, a government-issued photo ID (§ 316.212(7)). A town can add its own rules.

Does a golf cart need a permit or registration in Florida?

The state doesn’t register golf carts. Many towns and counties run their own registration or decal, so check the local page. LSVs must be titled, registered and insured with the state.

Where can golf carts go in Florida?

Only on roads a county or city has designated for golf carts, after deciding carts can use them safely, and where it has posted signs. Without a designation, golf carts aren’t allowed on public roads.

  • State highways: only to cross, where a designated road meets the highway and the state Department of Transportation has approved the crossing.
  • State park roads posted 35 mph or less, where the park system allows it.
  • Inside a self-contained retirement community, carts may be driven reasonably unless the county, city or state has prohibited it (§ 316.2125).

Local governments can be stricter than the state, and they can allow carts on sidewalks next to designated streets at 15 mph or less.

Golf cart or LSV: what’s the difference in Florida?

A golf cart is built for the golf course and can’t go faster than 20 mph. A low-speed vehicle has four wheels and a top speed over 20 mph and up to 25 (§ 320.01).

An LSV can use streets posted 35 mph or less, unless the county or city has closed a road to LSVs. An LSV can’t travel along roads posted over 35; lawful crossings are separately regulated. It must be titled, registered and insured, with a licensed driver (§ 316.2122).

The 35 mph rule is for LSVs only. It doesn’t open any road to golf carts.

Can you drive a golf cart at night in Florida? Hours and lights

Golf carts: sunrise to sunset. Night use is allowed only where the local government permits it and the cart has headlights, brake lights, turn signals and a windshield (§ 316.212(5)).

Crossing roads

Golf carts may cross a state highway only where a designated county road or city street meets it and the state has approved the crossing. A cart may also cross a road in the middle of a block between two parts of a golf course, and cross streets in a mobile home park at approved points.

Equipment

Every golf cart on a road needs efficient brakes, reliable steering, safe tires, a rearview mirror, and red reflectors on the front and rear (§ 316.212(6)). Night use adds headlights, brake lights, turn signals and a windshield.
